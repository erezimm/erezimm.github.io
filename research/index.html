Supernovae from the ground and from space

My research focuses on discovering and observing supernovae from the first hours after explosion to late phases. I am interested in constraining the properties of supernova progenitor stars, mapping their mass-loss history, and understanding supernova explosion mechanisms. In particular, I have studied nearby supernovae, including the decadal supernova 2023ixf (Zimmerman et al. 2024, published in Nature), for which I obtained HST UV spectra, and the extremely faint Iax supernova 2024vjm (Zimmerman et al., in review for Nature Astronomy), for which I analyzed a pre-explosion Euclid space telescope image containing the explosion site. I have led several successful telescope proposals with instruments such as the Hubble Space Telescope, Gemini, VLT, and others.

Collaborations

I am an active participant in major transient collaborations including LAST, BlackGEM, ePESSTO+, SoXS, ULTRASAT, and formerly ZTF, detecting, classifying and following-up interesting supernovae. I lead the BlackGEM consortium bi-weekly transient meetings, regularly participate in ePESSTO+ classifications as part of the TNS team, and developed CAST, a TOM-based transient marshal for the LAST collaboration. Since 2021 I have been a fellow of the Weizmann–Helmholtz Multi-Messenger School.
