Supernovae from the ground and from space

I study supernovae to constrain their progenitor stars, pre-explosion mass loss, and explosion mechanisms. In particular, I have studied nearby supernovae, including the decadal supernova 2023ixf (Zimmerman et al. 2024, published in Nature), for which I obtained first-of-its-kind ultraviolet spectroscopy using the Hubble Space Telescope, and the extremely faint Iax supernova 2024vjm (Zimmerman et al., accepted in principle at Nature Astronomy), for which I analyzed a pre-explosion Euclid space telescope image containing the explosion site. I have led several successful telescope proposals for facilities such as the Hubble Space Telescope, Gemini, and the VLT.

Collaborations

I am a member of major transient collaborations including BlackGEM, LAST, ePESSTO+, SoXS, ULTRASAT, and formerly ZTF. I lead the BlackGEM consortium bi-weekly transient meetings, and I developed CAST, a TOM-based transient marshal for the Weizmann Observatory's LAST collaboration. From 2021 to 2026 I was a fellow of the Weizmann–Helmholtz Multi-Messenger School.
